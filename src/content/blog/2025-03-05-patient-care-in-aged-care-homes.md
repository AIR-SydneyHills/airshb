Patient care in aged care homes involves a holistic approach that encompasses various aspects of a resident's well-being. This includes clinical care, assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs), and social and emotional support. There must be a collaborative effort of clinical and non-clinical staff in providing comprehensive patient care.

Clinical care involves:

·       Nursing care

·       Medication management

·       Access to medical services

Non-clinical staff contribute to clinical care by:

·       Assisting residents with taking pre-dispensed
medications

·       Monitoring and reporting changes in residents'
conditions to nursing staff

·       Providing personal care, including assistance
with showering, dressing, and grooming

·       Helping residents with mobility tasks

·       Providing social and emotional support

ADLs are basic self-care tasks that many residents may
need help with. These include:

·       Oral and dental care

·       Showering, bathing, dressing, and grooming

·       Mobility, including getting in and out of bed,
chairs, and wheelchairs

·       Eating and drinking

·       Personal hygiene

·       Toileting

·       Continence management

There is a significance of providing a safe and comfortable environment where residents receive respectful and compassionate care. Individualised care plans are crucial to ensure that patient care meets each resident's specific needs. Additionally, promoting social interaction and engagement contributes to residents' overall well-being.
